# Project Vision

## Purpose

Talisma SIS will be the institutional system of record for the higher-education student lifecycle. It will give students, faculty, advisors, registrars, finance teams, and administrators a consistent platform for academic operations while preserving accountability, privacy, and data integrity.

The product is not a collection of unrelated ERPNext customizations. Each capability must fit a shared academic model and contribute to a coherent, supportable SIS.

## Product outcomes

Talisma SIS should enable institutions to:

- manage applicants, admissions decisions, and student matriculation;
- maintain trusted biographical, academic, and program records;
- model curricula, courses, sections, terms, and registration rules;
- support advising, attendance, assessment, progression, and degree audit;
- produce controlled transcripts and graduation outcomes;
- integrate student billing and financial aid with financial operations;
- provide purpose-built student and faculty experiences;
- enforce FERPA-aligned access and maintain meaningful audit evidence; and
- deliver operational and strategic reporting without compromising record integrity.

## Product principles

1. **Student lifecycle first.** Features must align with an end-to-end academic journey rather than isolated screens.
2. **Institutional truth.** Every authoritative record must have clear ownership, lifecycle, validation, and audit behavior.
3. **Privacy by design.** Access to educational records must be intentional, minimal, and reviewable.
4. **Configuration over forks.** Institutional variation should be expressed through metadata and supported extension points.
5. **Upgrade safety.** Talisma code must remain separate from Frappe and ERPNext core.
6. **Operational clarity.** Workflows, exceptions, and approvals must be visible and explainable.
7. **Measured scalability.** Designs should support growth in institutions, campuses, programs, students, and integrations without premature complexity.

## Stakeholders

- Prospective students and applicants
- Students and alumni
- Faculty and instructors
- Academic advisors
- Admissions and registrar teams
- Curriculum and academic governance teams
- Student finance and financial-aid teams
- Institutional research and compliance teams
- System administrators and integration partners

## Success criteria

Success means institutions can operate critical academic processes with reliable data, controlled access, traceable decisions, predictable upgrades, and a user experience designed for higher education.

## Current boundary

This foundation phase establishes application ownership, architecture, engineering standards, and a phased roadmap. Functional models and interfaces will be introduced only after the relevant domain design is reviewed.
